Key insight: Every reversible inhibitor uses one master equation: $v_0 = \dfrac{V_{max}[S]}{\alpha K_m + \alpha'[S]}$. The only difference between types is which $\alpha$ values equal 1 vs. >1, and their relative sizes. Competitive: $\alpha' = 1$. Uncompetitive: $\alpha = 1$. Mixed/Noncompetitive: both > 1. Noncompetitive is just the special case where $\alpha = \alpha'$, so $V_{max}/\alpha' = V_{max}/\alpha$: they are the same thing.

Full Derivation from First Principles

Deriving the general mixed-inhibition rate equation without simplifying $\alpha$ or $\alpha'$

Step 1: Define the reaction scheme

Consider an enzyme $E$ that binds substrate $S$ to form an enzyme-substrate complex $ES$, which then breaks down to release product $P$ and regenerate free enzyme. A reversible inhibitor $I$ can bind to free enzyme (forming $EI$) and/or to the enzyme-substrate complex (forming $ESI$). Neither $EI$ nor $ESI$ produces product.
$E + S \underset{k_{-1}}{\overset{k_1}{\rightleftharpoons}} ES \overset{k_2}{\longrightarrow} E + P$
$E + I \underset{k_{-3}}{\overset{k_3}{\rightleftharpoons}} EI \qquad\qquad ES + I \underset{k_{-4}}{\overset{k_4}{\rightleftharpoons}} ESI$
We assume rapid equilibrium: the binding/unbinding steps are fast relative to catalysis ($k_2$), so the complexes $EI$ and $ESI$ are in true thermodynamic equilibrium. We also assume the steady-state approximation for $ES$ (its concentration does not change appreciably over time).

Step 2: Define equilibrium dissociation constants

From the equilibrium assumption, we define three dissociation constants:
$$K_m = \frac{k_{-1} + k_2}{k_1} \approx \frac{[E][S]}{[ES]} \quad \text{(Michaelis constant, from steady-state on } ES\text{)}$$ $$K_i = \frac{[E][I]}{[EI]} \quad \text{(inhibitor dissociation from free enzyme)}$$ $$K_i' = \frac{[ES][I]}{[ESI]} \quad \text{(inhibitor dissociation from ES complex)}$$
A smaller $K_i$ means tighter binding. If $K_i < K_i'$, the inhibitor has higher affinity for $E$ than for $ES$, and vice versa.

Step 3: Write the enzyme conservation equation

The total enzyme concentration $[E]_T$ is distributed across all four species:
$$[E]_T = [E] + [ES] + [EI] + [ESI]$$
Now express each species in terms of $[E]$ and measurable concentrations. From the equilibrium definitions:
$$[ES] = \frac{[E][S]}{K_m}$$ $$[EI] = \frac{[E][I]}{K_i}$$ $$[ESI] = \frac{[ES][I]}{K_i'} = \frac{[E][S][I]}{K_m K_i'}$$

Step 4: Substitute into the conservation equation

$$[E]_T = [E] + \frac{[E][S]}{K_m} + \frac{[E][I]}{K_i} + \frac{[E][S][I]}{K_m K_i'}$$
Factor out $[E]$:
$$[E]_T = [E]\!\left(1 + \frac{[S]}{K_m} + \frac{[I]}{K_i} + \frac{[S][I]}{K_m K_i'}\right)$$
Group the terms strategically to separate the substrate-dependent and substrate-independent parts:
$$[E]_T = [E]\!\left[\left(1 + \frac{[I]}{K_i}\right) + \frac{[S]}{K_m}\!\left(1 + \frac{[I]}{K_i'}\right)\right]$$

Step 5: Define $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$

To keep things clean, define two dimensionless grouping factors:
$$\alpha = 1 + \frac{[I]}{K_i} \qquad \alpha' = 1 + \frac{[I]}{K_i'}$$
So the conservation equation becomes:
$$[E]_T = [E]\!\left(\alpha + \frac{\alpha'[S]}{K_m}\right)$$
Solve for $[E]$:
$$[E] = \frac{[E]_T}{\alpha + \dfrac{\alpha'[S]}{K_m}} = \frac{[E]_T \cdot K_m}{\alpha K_m + \alpha'[S]}$$

Step 6: Write the rate equation

The reaction rate is determined only by the productive $ES$ complex breaking down:
$$v_0 = k_2[ES] = k_2 \cdot \frac{[E][S]}{K_m}$$
Substitute the expression for $[E]$ from Step 5:
$$v_0 = k_2 \cdot \frac{[S]}{K_m} \cdot \frac{[E]_T \cdot K_m}{\alpha K_m + \alpha'[S]}$$
The $K_m$ terms cancel:
$$v_0 = \frac{k_2[E]_T[S]}{\alpha K_m + \alpha'[S]}$$
Recognize that $k_2[E]_T = V_{max}$ (the maximum rate when all enzyme is saturated with substrate and no inhibitor is present):
$$\boxed{v_0 = \frac{V_{max}[S]}{\alpha K_m + \alpha'[S]}}$$

Step 7: Fully expanded form (no $\alpha$ shorthand)

Substituting back the definitions of $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$:
$$v_0 = \frac{V_{max}[S]}{\left(1 + \dfrac{[I]}{K_i}\right)K_m + \left(1 + \dfrac{[I]}{K_i'}\right)[S]}$$
Expanding the denominator fully:
$$v_0 = \frac{V_{max}[S]}{K_m + \dfrac{[I]\,K_m}{K_i} + [S] + \dfrac{[I][S]}{K_i'}}$$
This is the most general form. Each inhibitor type just removes terms. Competitive: $K_i' \to \infty$, so $\frac{[I][S]}{K_i'} \to 0$. Uncompetitive: $K_i \to \infty$, so $\frac{[I]K_m}{K_i} \to 0$. Noncompetitive: $K_i = K_i'$, so the two inhibitor terms combine symmetrically.

Step 8: Lineweaver-Burk form

Take the reciprocal of both sides of the master equation:
$$\frac{1}{v_0} = \frac{\alpha K_m + \alpha'[S]}{V_{max}[S]} = \frac{\alpha K_m}{V_{max}} \cdot \frac{1}{[S]} + \frac{\alpha'}{V_{max}}$$
This is in the form $y = mx + b$ where $y = 1/v_0$ and $x = 1/[S]$:
$$\text{Slope} = \frac{\alpha K_m}{V_{max}} = \frac{K_m}{V_{max}}\!\left(1 + \frac{[I]}{K_i}\right) \qquad \text{Y-intercept} = \frac{\alpha'}{V_{max}} = \frac{1}{V_{max}}\!\left(1 + \frac{[I]}{K_i'}\right)$$
Competitive: only slope changes (same y-intercept). Uncompetitive: only y-intercept changes (same slope, parallel lines). Mixed: both change. Noncompetitive: both change by the same factor (lines cross on the x-axis).

Step 9: Finding the Lineweaver-Burk intersection point

Set the uninhibited L-B equation equal to the inhibited one and solve for $x = 1/[S]$:
$$\frac{K_m}{V_{max}} \cdot x + \frac{1}{V_{max}} = \frac{\alpha K_m}{V_{max}} \cdot x + \frac{\alpha'}{V_{max}}$$
Cancel $1/V_{max}$ and solve:
$$K_m \cdot x + 1 = \alpha K_m \cdot x + \alpha'$$ $$K_m \cdot x\,(1 - \alpha) = \alpha' - 1$$ $$x^* = \frac{\alpha' - 1}{K_m(1 - \alpha)} = -\frac{1}{K_m}\cdot\frac{\alpha' - 1}{\alpha - 1}$$
Substitute back to find $y^*$:
$$y^* = \frac{1}{V_{max}}\!\left(1 - \frac{\alpha' - 1}{\alpha - 1}\right) = \frac{1}{V_{max}}\cdot\frac{\alpha - \alpha'}{\alpha - 1}$$
If $\alpha > \alpha'$ (prefers E, competitive lean): $y^* > 0$, intersection above x-axis.
If $\alpha < \alpha'$ (prefers ES, uncompetitive lean): $y^* < 0$, intersection below x-axis.
If $\alpha = \alpha'$ (noncompetitive): $y^* = 0$, intersection exactly on x-axis at $x = -1/K_m$.
